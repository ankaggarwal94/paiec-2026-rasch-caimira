# Training across benchmark families

This development study asks whether additional training families improve prediction on the same unseen family. The earlier two-benchmark embedding experiment and published scores retain their original population. This protocol precedes the new fits. The organizer's [data and evaluation rules](https://aims.stanford.edu/competition) define the permitted sources and scoring target.

Use inventory-listed sources with documented use, identifiable subjects, visible item inputs and observed binary grades for individual attempts. Count unavailable grades separately. Missing grades cannot become successes or failures. Prefer a fuller verified source for the same suite over an overlapping archival slice. Record the exact source scope and exclusions. Keep previously reserved EngDesign, MTBbench and DAVE closed pending evidence of their exposure. The resulting folds are development data.

Rotate each family through outer evaluation. Group benchmark aliases and related versions before training, including SWE-bench and SWE-rebench when both are present. Purge every training source group containing an item whose normalized text matches any item in the held family. Training can use pairs with fewer than 80 items. Score every pair with at least 80 distinct visible items. For each pair, split distinct items in half using an outcome-independent hash. Keep all repeats of an item together. Choose one recorded trial per purchased item by hash. Use three fixed histories, 20261030 through 20261032. All predictors see the same nested purchases at budgets 0, 1, 3, 7, 15 and 31. Report differences from hosted sampling where item aliases collapse. The inference task is to forecast the probability of a binary outcome from visible attributes and allowed evidence.

Compare training on one family, three families and every other family. For each held family, order the others by independently seeded reproducible hash and take nested prefixes. Rotate the small training subsets across three draws to reduce dependence on a lucky donor. A learning curve at these fixed test targets estimates the effect of increasing both data and family diversity together. Exclude exact duplicates of a fit job. Use fixed model settings, with seed 17 as the deployment replicate and seeds 29 and 1013 for stochastic sensitivity. No settings are tuned on the outer test family.

Include the raw purchased-label mean and its Beta(4,4) smoothed version (PM8). Test fold-trained lexical logistic, Rasch, CAIMIRA, NCF and EntityMarformer predictors. For learned models score zero-label forecasts unchanged and with an intercept fitted to purchased labels only, using a fixed unit-normal prior. Report the broad feature representation and actual architecture behind every name. Previously trained checkpoints require a separate overlap assessment. Present other procedures separately if their source features, acquisition policy or authorized inference budget differ. Acquisition comparisons must start from identical streams and use their own purchases.

The primary endpoint is the official-style Brier area under the learning curve, averaged equally over eligible subject–benchmark pairs and then over histories and training replicates. Report the curve at each budget, scores per family, and an equal-family secondary average. Retain all held-out trials for scored items. Use paired family resampling to describe uncertainty across observed families, conditioning on trained procedures. For local final refitting, select the full-pool method by development pair-weighted Brier ALC from these declared candidates, with PM8 as the lower-complexity tie break. Distinguish this development selection from external validation and from the incumbent's separate promotion gates. A private hosted score can be obtained only from the organizer. Record fit, score, refit and reload results separately.

Implementation decisions for the wider run were frozen before its model fits. In addition to common fixed and intercept-adjusted forecasts, Rasch and CAIMIRA update latent ability from the purchased outcomes and the relational model attends to them directly. Include each of those native updates in the comparison. Lexical ridge fitting is deterministic. For stochastic fits pair donor draws 37, 83 and 151 with fit seeds 17, 29 and 1013 respectively, and fit all three seeds independently on the full training fold. Reuse the exact same histories across draws. Visible item identity uses the normalized text and supplied feature and interactor strings. Connect variants sharing a native item ID and items sharing a visible tuple within a pair. Each scored pair needs at least 80 such allocation units. The MMLU-derived uncertainty suite shares an outer fold with MMLU-Pro, Multi-SWE-bench with SWE-rebench, and tau-bench with tau2. These are source-lineage families. There are many more task types than source lineages, and broad domain transfer requires a separate grouping.

The combined catalog-v5 run adds explicit common ancestry for JETTS, MoE-CAP and MMLU-derived sources through GSM8K and MMLU, VHELM and MathVista Mini through MathVista, and SWE-bench Pro through the SWE benchmark family. Its supplements use original, attributable trial records for AndroidWorld, CYBENCH, MIR-Bench and SWE-bench Pro. For MIR, the published executable grader defines the primary binary label. Its optional parser references a missing import, and enabling it changes 515 of 12,000 outcomes. Preserve this uncertainty in interpretation. For SWE-bench Pro retain the raw attempt UUIDs and reported success booleans. For CYBENCH exclude fractional aggregates. Existing scored local subsets remain exposed development data. The complete comparison includes every reviewed and admitted trial of each frozen source. Public availability of a benchmark paper or question bank alone cannot establish a model response.

The final source freeze adds 13,000 individually graded MedHELM predictions on MedCalc-Bench. MedCalc and MedAgentBoard share a holdout family because both use source material from MedQA. Forty-four MedCalc items matched full patient notes with confirmed USMLE source identifiers. Together the 44 suites supply 10,951,005 eligible attempts across 36 source lineages.
